SLIDE 6: PLANE 2 - QUANTUM MACHINE LEARNING & AUTOENCODER PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Layout]: Data flow schematic: 99 features -> PyTorch AE (64, 32, 6) -> Angle Embedding -> 6-Qubit VQC Ansatz -> Pauli-Z -> Softmax.
[Slide Content]:
  - The Dimensionality Challenge: 99 features cannot be directly encoded into NISQ quantum registers without severe noise and decoherence.
  - Autoencoder Architecture:
    * Encoder: Linear(99, 64) -> ReLU -> Linear(64, 32) -> ReLU -> Linear(32, 6) -> Sigmoid bottleneck.
    * MinMax scaling into [0, pi] domain strictly fitted on training splits to prevent data leakage.
  - 6-Qubit Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC):
    * Quantum Device: PennyLane default.qubit simulator.
    * Data Encoding: Single-qubit rotation gates RY(theta_i = z_i * pi) across 6 qubits.
    * Parameterized Ansatz: L=2 entangling layers with strongly entangling CNOT rings and trainable RY/RZ rotations (72 quantum parameters).
    * Measurement: Expectation value of Pauli-Z operators <psi| Z_i |psi>, mapped via classical linear head (70 parameters) to class probabilities.
[Speaker Notes]:
  "In Plane 2, our primary goal was quantum parameter efficiency without succumbing to Barren Plateaus. Our PyTorch autoencoder compresses 99 features down to 6 highly dense, non-linear latent variables. These are embedded as rotation angles onto a 6-qubit register. With just two entangling layers and 142 total parameters, the circuit computes Pauli-Z expectation values to determine attack probabilities."

[Speaker Notes]:
  "We didn't just build the winning model—we mathematically proved why alternative architectures fail. A naive 16-qubit circuit suffered complete gradient collapse due to barren plateaus. QSVM was computationally impossible at scale. And linear PCA destroyed vital non-linear relationships. Our deep autoencoder recovered over 17 percentage points in accuracy, validating our architectural choice."

[Slide Content]:

    * High-impact containment actions (e.g., core database isolation, firewall drops) trigger a conditional LangGraph pause gate.

[Speaker Notes]:

Second, for critical containment actions, LangGraph halts execution and presents an interactive human-in-the-loop authorization gate, giving human analysts final veto power."

[Slide Content]:

  - Real-Time Channel Integrity Monitoring:
    * Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER) continuously tracked: QBER = E_error / N_sample.
    * Nominal fiber transmission: QBER = 3.42% (< 11% theoretical threshold).
  - Sub-10ms Cryptographic Failover (TC-10):
    * If eavesdropping spikes QBER above threshold (e.g., 28.5%), the quantum link immediately terminates.
    * Seamless sub-10ms failover to NIST FIPS 203 ML-KEM-768 (Kyber) encapsulated AES-256-GCM session keys; 0 packet loss.
[Speaker Notes]:

If an attacker attempts to intercept photon states, the QBER spikes above 11%. Our system instantly terminates the channel and fails over within 8 milliseconds to NIST FIPS 203 ML-KEM post-quantum keys without dropping a single packet."
